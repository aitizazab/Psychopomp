# Baja's voice: direction notes for a human recording

Baja, the God of Death. Whoever records her (the creator, a teammate or a friend) should read this once, then do
each line two or three times. Candidate TTS voices and the qualities they aim for are on the review page
`FableAnimationTests/art/review/2026-10-18/baja-voice.html`. The lines live in `docs/HELL-SCRIPT.md` (speaker `GOD`).

**Not an impression.** The creator hears her as "a version of Mara Sov's voice" (Destiny 2). Use that only for the
qualities below. Don't imitate the actress: the repo is public, and a real person's voice likeness needs her consent.

## Who she is, in one breath
The most powerful being in the room. She's bored, and she's found something funny: you. She never needs to raise
her voice, because nothing can make her hurry. The menace comes from how relaxed she is.

## Pitch and placement
- Low-to-mid, settled in the chest. If you have a range, use its lower comfortable third. Don't push down into
  gravel or a "villain" growl.
- Stay level. She doesn't swoop up at the ends of sentences, and her questions barely rise ("Well?" goes down).

## Pace
- Unhurried: about 10-15% slower than you'd say it naturally. Let full stops land and take a real beat at "..."
  (half a second or more).
- Short commands are short and final: "Up." "Fetch it." "Permanently." One word, then nothing.
- Never rush the end of a line to get to the joke. The pause before it is the joke.

## Diction
- Crisp consonants, finished word endings (the t in "fruit", the d in "idea" if it's there). A light, slightly formal
  polish, as if she learned English centuries ago and kept it pressed.

## Where the smile goes
- Smile physically on the amused words. It changes the sound. Leave a straight face on the threats.
  - "...Wait. I've had the **funniest** idea." Straight on "Wait", then the smile arrives on "funniest".
  - "Make it stop. **Permanently.**" A pleased, light "permanently", as if she's choosing a dessert.
  - "Well? Chop chop. **I have a lunch.**" Bored. "Lunch" is the most important thing she's said all day.
  - "Shadows are doors, **if you're mine.**" Intimate, nearly a whisper, a little possessive.
  - "No, no. You die when **I** say. Up." Lean on "I" without getting louder. "Up" is flat, like waking a dog.
  - "...And you did every one of my chores. **Ha!**" The only real laugh: one short, delighted, genuine "Ha!", not
    a cackle.
- CAPS in the script (YOU, HELL, SMALL) are leaned on with weight and a slower word, never with volume.
- Never shout. If a line feels like it needs volume, go quieter and closer instead.

## Mic and room
- Close: 10-15 cm from the mic (a hand's width), slightly off-axis (aim the mic at the corner of your mouth) so the
  p's and b's don't pop. Some breath on the edges is good: it's part of the intimacy.
- A dead room: a closet full of clothes, or a duvet over your head and the phone. No echo of your own, because we add
  the echo afterwards.
- Phone voice memo or any USB mic is fine. Record WAV or the best quality setting, peaks well below the top
  (around -10 dB), and a second of silence before and after each take.
- Name the files by line key (`open-04-take2.wav`).

## What happens after
We run the takes through the same chain as the candidates: a little low warmth, presence, a faint double voice a few
cents off and a few milliseconds late, and a short, dark, cathedral-like tail, all levelled to -18 LUFS. So record
it dry and natural; the godlike part is added in post.
